Title: The Icon of Man 🚹
Date: 2026/10/04 05:49PM
Category: Short Stories
Author: Mike Russell

There I was, breathlessly running to an appointment whose urgency and untimeliness could hardly be understated. My hair did not sway softly in the breeze as I walked but stood resolutely, stoically, as if to encourage me to do the same, as if to say, "onward, brave soldier". A cold bead of sweat betrayed my confidence.

I hoped onlookers wouldn't notice as I passed through the city's labyrinth of concrete corridors. I paid them tacit greetings with a swift, brief nod and a quick glance—eye contact enough to convey normalcy and decency, but short enough to avoid diverting my mission.

"Can they sense my dilemma?" I wondered, hoping against all odds that they could not, contemplating the disconcertment such would cost me. I was able to jettison this question only because of my desperation—"I haven't the luxury to concern myself with that", I muttered wistfully, mused by the sardonic irony in which I waded.

I cast a furtive gaze toward the sign marking my destination. The icon it touted was crafted with childlike simplicity. It was received as unsympathetic ridicule, an insolent slap to the face. In no way did it resemble the complex and agonizing pain I suffered.

As I approached, I marvelled at the enigma this place was: a place both of pain and relief, but mostly of quietude, like a church. Yet the ambience had no reverence, rather, awkward discomfort. Here the activities its occupants labored were noise enough: eerie echoes, footsteps, a rush of water, a dispensing of hand-drying equipment, a door whose slam comes much louder than expected, whispered apologies—spoken to please the conscience but not mean to be heard. Perhaps some would force a polite smile or remark, but most would keep to themselves, knowing silence was the best defense against a regretted faux pas.

I resolutely pushed open the door upon arrival. The smell was remarkable but, owing to its pedestrian abundance, would be easily and willingly forgotten. An enclave was available for a suitor—that would be me. I entered. What followed was instant relief.

I pondered in silent meditation, eyes closed, fingers restfully interlaced. Perhaps it was not wrong to compare this dwelling to a church after all. Like a sanctuary of solitude, it was to me. The relentless pressures of my world were hushed. For a moment, I had the blissful lightness of infancy.

The dullness of the four walls in which I sat drew my eyes to the floor. I admired the geometric tiles which covered it, protecting it, much like a tortoise's shell. The floor was still wet, wet from its regular cleaning, I hoped. I marvelled at how the sterile aroma and subtle residual bubbles were insufficient to convince me it was clean, however. I dared not let anything but the undersurface of the soles of my shoes.

Soon I emptied the room of my occupancy. In reality, it had emptied me of more burdens than I realized. With deep gratitude, I thanked that icon of a man upon the sign—the man whom I had so recently resented for his simplicity. Though, it had seemed a mockery then, it now made sense: what his austerity represented was a resistance to the growing hustle and bustle of life, a yearning for what once was, the simple way of man, an apt reminder that we are still just humans.

He now seemed more like a cave painting made by primitive man: ancient wisdom. "Thank you" was the only fitting remark to the small but profound symbol. It was as if I heard his gracious reply, "And thank you for your patronage, and that of all mankind." Though this experience would soon fade into the forgotten archives of my mind, my heart would forever be changed, probably, at least.
